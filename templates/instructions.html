Place Similarity Study

About this study

We are researchers at MIT studying how people judge whether two places look and feel alike. You will compare street scenes from a city and tell us how similar they seem to you.

What you will do

  • At the top you will see a reference place (a street-level photo).
  • Below it you will see another place as a 360° view: four photos taken from the exact same spot, each looking in a different direction (ahead, right, behind, left), so together they show everything around that one spot. Compare mainly the view marked with an arrow; the other three show what is around it. Hover over any image to see it larger.
  • Use the slider to rate how similar it is to the reference, from 1 (not at all similar) to 7 (very similar), then press Next. If you like, add a short note on why in the box below the slider (optional).
  • You will start with a short practice round of 4 comparisons to get used to the page. These don't count.
  • Then there are 3 reference places, each compared with 22 places (66 ratings in total). The reference stays the same until it changes to the next one.

What to focus on

  • Judge the place itself: the kind of street, the buildings, trees and greenery, how open or enclosed it feels, and its overall character.
  • Try to ignore the weather, season and time of day, individual cars or people, and photo quality.
  • There are no right or wrong answers. Go with your first impression and please spend no more than about 20 seconds on each comparison.

Practical details

  • The study takes about 25–35 minutes. Please use a laptop or desktop computer.
  • You can press Back to change an answer. Your answers are saved as you go, so if you close the tab by accident, open the study link again to continue where you left off.
  • At the end you will get a completion code for Prolific.
